What this is, and what it is not

This is a scan, dated and bounded, rather than a directory of streaming vendors. Every record comes from one of three places: a pitch that reached my inbox between June and 8 September 2026, the official IBC directory listing for Hall 5, or research I did on a company I wanted to understand. Seventy-three of the hundred emailed me, twenty-six never got in touch and are here for comparison, and one is an advisory client of mine.

The selection method is the thing to hold in mind while reading. An inbox is a record of who spent money on public relations, so it over-represents vendors with agencies and under-represents everyone else. The official Hall 5 list makes the size of that skew visible: of 162 exhibitors in the hall, 138 never contacted me, and several of those have stronger live-sport deployments than the ones who did. Hall 5 itself is one hall out of more than fourteen, against a show with over 1,300 exhibitors, so most of IBC2026 sits outside this scan. Eighty-one of the companies here have a confirmed stand, spread across eight halls, and only forty-four of those are in Hall 5.

Each record carries a confidence level, shown as a coloured dot at its foot. The level is calculated from the evidence behind the record: two points for a record built from the full text of a message or from a demonstration, one for a record built from public sources, none for one built from a subject line; then a point each for post-show coverage found, a checkable number offered, and a booth code confirmed against the directory or the company's own message. Four or five is high, two or three is medium, nought or one is low. Twenty-eight are high, fifty-one medium and twenty-one low, and the sourcing behind each one is written out beneath it.

Depth varies and the records say so. Thirty-nine were built from reading a message in full or from a demonstration, thirty-five from public sources, and twenty-six from a subject line, a snippet or a directory blurb. Those last are provisional, and several have already turned out to be wrong once further evidence arrived. Booth codes carry their source, and any traced back to a 2025 floor plan have been removed.

Two passes are complete across every record. Each carries a launch classification, and each has been searched for post-show coverage, with some yielding an entry and many returning nothing findable. Where a record says no launch on the strength of an empty search rather than evidence from the stand, that is a weaker call and the sourcing note says so.

The plan is to widen this into something closer to a proper vendor database, with a defined universe, a source stack beyond my own inbox and a refresh cadence that survives the current rate of consolidation. That is a different project and it starts from a written definition of what counts as a streaming vendor. Until then this stays what it says on the heading, which is a scan of one show from one vantage point.
