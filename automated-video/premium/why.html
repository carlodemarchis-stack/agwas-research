Why the camera is on the tripod and the match is still not online

The technology for filming youth football without an operator is cheap and it works. In Italy the match still stays on the pitch, and the reasons are written in three documents nobody at the gate has read.

My son plays in a Juventus Academy Elite club in Turin, and most Sundays I record his matches with an XbotGo Falcon on a tripod behind the goal, a camera that cost €869 on the Italian store and asks for no subscription it-11. Parents from both teams ask me the same thing on the touchline: why is this not just there, at every pitch, the way it is for a Danish club or an American high school? For a year my answer was that the technology is there, the need is there, and something in the business plan does not work. That answer was right and it was lazy, so I went and read the documents.

The short version is that the camera is the solved part. Veo, the Danish company that sells the most common club camera in Europe, closed 2025 with DKK 504 million of revenue and a net loss of DKK 4.6 million after losing DKK 168 million two years earlier, according to its filed accounts veo-08, which is what a working business model looks like. Pixellot has 16,000 systems in American high schools and moved its whole contract with the schools' network to a revenue share in November 2025 it-29. Sportway in Stockholm produces 250,000 matches a year from 2,500 cameras and raised €20 million in June 2026 to buy more of Europe it-30. Three different models, each with accounts or a contract behind it. In Italy three locks hold the pitch closed, and each one is a rule or an incentive.

The first lock is the LND circular that forbids the model that works elsewhere

The regional youth championships, Under 15 and Under 17 included, are run by the Comitati Regionali and the Delegazioni of the Lega Nazionale Dilettanti under guidelines from the FIGC youth sector, whose own rulebook for 2026/27 says nothing about video at all it-06. The rules on streaming therefore sit in three LND circulars reissued on 1 July 2026. Circolare n. 8 lets a club stream its home matches, on its own official social accounts, free of charge to the viewer, and it forbids every other method, names "interconnessioni con operatori della comunicazione" as banned, and sends offenders to the Procura Federale it-01. Circolare n. 7 keeps the audiovisual rights with the LND, prices live coverage of the national competitions at €250 to €500 a match, and for regional and provincial matches allows an outside live only "per motivi e circostanze particolari", paid, on terms set case by case by LND Servizi it-02. Circolare n. 6 caps the press at three minutes of delayed footage and states that the right to report "non si può identificare in nessun caso con la diffusione in diretta delle immagini delle gare" it-03.

Read that against the vendor map on this site and the consequence is plain. The model that produces an audience abroad is a federation or a platform that pays for the cameras, collects every match into one app and sells a season pass with a cut to the club: the Swedish FA's Min Fotboll charges 59 SEK for 30 days of every youth match it streams and pays 50% of that, after VAT, to the club the viewer picks it-16; the NFHS Network in the US charges $79.99 a year and pays $20 of each annual subscription to the school the subscriber names it-20. A club cannot build that in Italy under Circolare n. 8, because the authorisation demands the club's own social page and a price of zero, and a paid central service would need a different rights agreement with the LND, which I have not found. The regime itself was born as an "autorizzazione straordinaria" on 1 October 2020 so that supporters could follow matches played behind closed doors it-04, and six years later the emergency exception is the whole framework, with Serie D the one level where paid social streams have since been admitted it-05.

The second lock is consent, per child, per match, both teams

The FIGC safeguarding policy requires a signed parental liberatoria for any photo or video of a minor that is kept or used, section 2.7.1 it-07. A tracking camera records every player on the pitch, so the host club has to make sure the consents it holds cover the visiting team's children and this specific use of the pictures. Norway ran into that wall in January 2023, when Oslo, Bergen and other large municipalities refused MyGame cameras in halls used by under-18s it-19; the settlement is streaming from the 15-year age class with an opt-out handled through the federation's Min Idrett system, and MyGame now runs about 500 cameras for around 8,000 matches a year, by its own count it-17. Italy has not had that argument yet, and I have not found anyone who has tried at scale.

The third lock is that the gate is the club's only match-day money

My son's club charges €6 full and €4 reduced for youth and academy matches in 2026/27, and €12 for the Promozione first team it-12. Tickets of €3 to €6 at Under 15 to Under 17 level have produced local rows in Cremona, where clubs blamed energy bills and the cost of the riforma del lavoro sportivo it-13, in Lodi, where a regional FIGC youth coordinator said on record that no rule governs admission to youth matches it-15, and in Brescia this April it-14. So the club's Sunday income is the gate, the stream must by rule be free, and it goes out on a Facebook page the club does not monetise. Every incentive points the club away from the camera, and a treasurer who declines to buy one is being sensible. In Sweden the season pass pays the club 50%, in Norway 20%, in Georgia $20 a subscriber. In Italy the club would get zero and risk the €6.

What the volume says

Italian football played 586,929 official matches in 2023/24, one every 54 seconds, on 15,014 pitches, with close to 900,000 registered youth players, which is 22.7% of every Italian aged 5 to 16, all from the FIGC's own ReportCalcio 2025 it-21. The demand side is enormous. On the supply side, a 2023 survey of 1,029 facility operators by Svimez, UISP and Sport e Salute found 54% of them over thirty years old and 20% built after 2010 it-22, and I have not found public data on pitch-side connectivity at all. The pitches my son plays on look like the ones I played on.

Two facts from Italy point in opposite directions. Pixellot streamed 114 matches of the Torneo delle Regioni in Piemonte in April 2023, an LND production it-23; the 2026 edition in Puglia put 40 of 148 matches online with a conventional crew it-24. Automated coverage of the LND's own showcase went backwards. And Veo has been the LND's official partner since 8 September 2025, with an Italian market manager and discounts for affiliated clubs it-09, which is a crack in the wall, sold as a discount on a camera and carrying no distribution rights.

The position

The technology question is closed. The Italian question is who is allowed to build the layer that turns roughly 12,000 matches a weekend, my arithmetic from the LND's own 583,290 matches a season it-28, into something parents can watch and clubs can earn from, and today the answer is nobody, by rule. Whoever negotiates the next step, a federation app with a season pass and a revenue share back to the club, will own this space in Italy, and the vendors in the database below are the people who will be bidding for it.
