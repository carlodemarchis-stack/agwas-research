Swiss sportstech and sport governance: the directory

Three lists built around the Swiss SportsTech Report 2026: the 46 companies the report names, with segment, town and website; the 67 international federations and sport governance bodies based in Switzerland, with street address; and the 42 national federations, leagues, event owners, broadcasters, betting operators and agencies that make up the rest of the Swiss sports economy. Addresses checked in November 2026 against each organisation's own pages.

How to read this

The company list is the illustrative sample the report itself publishes, around fifty of the 402 it mapped, chosen by the authors to show breadth rather than rank. Descriptions are condensed from the report and from the companies' own pages where the report was thin. Two websites are left blank because I could not confirm a domain.

The federation list takes the wide view: Olympic and recognised federations, continental confederations, umbrella bodies and agencies. The report counts around 60, the Lausanne Olympic Capital Foundation says more than 70, and the difference is what you count. Four entries changed address or announced a move in the last twelve months and the table says which.

The third list exists because the buyers a Swiss sportstech company meets first are usually a national federation, a league, an event with a broadcast contract, or one of the agencies that sell rights for all of them. Federations move more often than people assume. Check before you post a letter.

Two things stood out compiling this part. Much of the Swiss rights business sits in two postcodes, Zug and Lucerne, far from Lausanne, which says something about where the money and the governance actually meet. And the biggest Swiss agency contract of the last thirty years, TEAM's UEFA mandate, ends in 2027 in favour of a US firm, and the report does not mention it, even though it is the clearest recent example of the commercial layer the authors say Switzerland lacks.
